          How the count is computed

          Each subset of pieces that could be a prefix of a legal dawn gets one number: how many ways to reach it. A piece may be added only when everything it waits on is already inside. The number on the full set is the count of topological sorts, the linear extensions of this partial order. With no bearings the bench uses n! directly, which is the same number the table would give. A ring short-circuits the table: the count is zero. Above 20 pieces the table is refused.

          Where else this pattern shows up
          • A build compiles a file after the files it imports.
          • A spreadsheet recomputes a cell after the cells it reads.
          • A curriculum offers a course after its prerequisites.
          • A migration creates a table after the tables it points to.
          • A frame of a game runs a pass after the passes that fill its inputs.
          • A craft unlocks a recipe after the recipes that produce its ingredients.

          The harbor gate, the orrery, and the press are the same ledger. If the arrows form a loop, there is no legal order. Finding the loop is the useful half of the algorithm.
